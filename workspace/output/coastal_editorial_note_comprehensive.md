## Chapter Structure & Organization

### Scope Decision

The coastal content was initially proposed as two separate chapters:
1. **Living Systems**: Coral reef ecosystems, intertidal zones, mangrove forests
2. **Physical Processes**: Coastal erosion, sedimentation, sea level change

**Decision (January 15)**: Keep as a single chapter with clear section breaks.

**Rationale**: Splitting would impact word count allocation and collection pricing. Organizing within a single chapter with distinct sections preserves editorial flexibility while maintaining thematic coherence.

## Synthesis & Observations

The coastal chapters (1-3) form a coherent narrative arc:
- **Ch. 1** establishes why coastal ecology matters (scope, ecosystem services)
- **Ch. 2** surveys the biological communities that inhabit these environments
- **Ch. 3** explains the physical processes that shape and sustain them

This progression allows readers to understand both *what* lives in coastal systems and *how* those systems are maintained. The decision to keep these as three chapters (rather than the initially proposed single mega-chapter or overseparated split) provides good balance.

**Data Currency**: The team's staged approach to data integration—completing structure first, then incorporating recent findings—is sound editorial practice. The coral bleaching section will benefit from the most recent 2024 monitoring data, and sea level projections are updated with latest IPCC assessments.
