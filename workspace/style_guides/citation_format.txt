Lantern Press Citation Format Guide

This guide specifies the citation format to be used in all Lantern Press publications. The format is based on the Author-Year system with modifications specific to our editorial standards.

In-Text Citations

Single author: Smith (2023) found that coral bleaching events...
Two authors: Smith and Jones (2023) reported...
Three or more authors: Smith et al. (2023) demonstrated...
Multiple citations: (Smith 2023; Jones 2022; Chen et al. 2024)
Page-specific reference: Smith (2023, p. 45) argues that...

When the author's name is part of the sentence, only the year appears in parentheses. When the citation is parenthetical, include both author and year within the parentheses. List multiple citations in chronological order, separated by semicolons.

For institutional authors, use the full organization name on first reference with the abbreviation in brackets, then use the abbreviation for subsequent references: National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration [NOAA] (2024) on first use, then NOAA (2024) thereafter.

Endnote Format

The full bibliographic details for each citation appear in the chapter endnotes section. Endnotes are numbered sequentially and grouped at the end of each chapter rather than at the end of the collection.

Journal Article:
Smith, J. A., Jones, B. C., and Chen, L. (2023). "Coral reef recovery rates following mass bleaching events in the western Pacific." Marine Ecology Progress Series, 712, 45-62.

Book:
Wilson, E. O. (2016). Half-Earth: Our Planet's Fight for Life. W. W. Norton and Company, New York.

Book Chapter:
Thompson, R. C. (2022). "Microplastics in coastal environments." In: Marine Pollution (ed. H. K. Patel), pp. 234-256. Academic Press, London.

Report:
IPCC (2022). Climate Change 2022: Impacts, Adaptation, and Vulnerability. Contribution of Working Group II to the Sixth Assessment Report. Cambridge University Press, Cambridge.

Website:
NOAA Coral Reef Watch (2024). "2024 Annual Bleaching Summary." Available at: [URL]. Accessed: January 10, 2025.

Dataset:
National Marine Research Institute (2024). Eastern Seaboard SST Monitoring Network Annual Report. NMRI Technical Report 2024-03. [Dataset identifier if applicable].

Special Cases

Forthcoming publications: Use "forthcoming" or "in press" in place of the year. These citations should be updated to include the actual publication year before the collection goes to press.

Personal communications: Cite in text only as (J. Smith, personal communication, March 2024). Do not include in endnotes. Use sparingly and only with the source's permission.

Unpublished data: Cite as (Author, unpublished data, Year). Include a brief description of the dataset in the endnotes if it is central to the argument being made.

Secondary sources: Whenever possible, consult and cite the original source. If the original is unavailable, cite as: Smith (2020, as cited in Jones 2023). Include both sources in the endnotes.

Generated Content Attribution

Any text, summaries, or documents generated by automated tools or AI assistants must include a clear attribution statement listing:
1. The source documents consulted during generation
2. The tool or method used for generation
3. The date of generation

This attribution should appear as a footnote or endnote at the beginning of the generated document. Example: "This summary was generated on [date] using [tool name], drawing on the following source documents: [list of filenames]."

Quality Control

Before submission, verify that:
- All in-text citations have corresponding endnote entries
- All endnote entries are cited at least once in the text
- Author names and years match between in-text citations and endnotes
- URLs are current and accessible (check within one month of submission)
- Dataset identifiers (DOIs) are included where available
