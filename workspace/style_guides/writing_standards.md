# Lantern Press Writing Standards

This document establishes the editorial standards for all publications in the Lantern Press coastal ecology collection. Contributors and editors should refer to these guidelines throughout the writing, editing, and production process.

## Voice and Tone

The collection should be accessible to educated general readers while maintaining scientific accuracy. Avoid unnecessary jargon, but do not shy away from technical terminology when it is the most precise way to express a concept. When introducing technical terms, provide a brief definition or contextual explanation on first use.

Write in active voice wherever possible. Passive constructions are acceptable when the agent is unknown, unimportant, or when emphasis should fall on the action rather than the actor. Vary sentence length to maintain reader engagement — short sentences for emphasis, longer sentences for complex explanations.

Maintain an objective, authoritative tone throughout. The collection informs rather than advocates, though it may note the scientific consensus on topics such as climate change without false balance. Personal anecdotes from researchers or field workers may be included as sidebars but should not dominate the main text.

## Structure and Organization

### Chapter Structure
Each chapter should follow this general structure:
1. Opening hook — a compelling image, fact, or question that draws the reader in
2. Context and scope — what this chapter covers and how it connects to the broader collection
3. Main content — organized in logical sections with clear headings
4. Synthesis — bringing together the chapter's themes and pointing toward future directions
5. Further reading — a short annotated list of accessible sources for interested readers

### Section Length
Individual sections within a chapter should be between five hundred and fifteen hundred words. Longer sections should be subdivided. The reader should be able to find specific information quickly through clear headings and logical organization.

### Headings
Use sentence case for all headings. Chapter titles use Title Case. Limit heading depth to three levels (chapter heading, section heading, subsection heading). Deeper structure should be achieved through paragraph organization rather than additional heading levels.

## Scientific Content

### Accuracy and Currency
All factual claims should be supported by peer-reviewed literature or authoritative institutional reports published within the last ten years unless referring to foundational concepts or historical data. When newer data supersedes older estimates, use the most recent values and note the change where relevant.

### Uncertainty and Qualification
Scientific writing requires appropriate qualification of claims. Use hedging language ("may," "suggests," "approximately") when evidence is incomplete or contested. Present ranges rather than single values when uncertainty is significant. Distinguish between well-established findings and emerging research.

### Numbers and Units
Use SI units throughout, with Imperial equivalents in parentheses only where they aid comprehension for a general audience. Spell out numbers below ten in running text; use numerals for ten and above. Always use numerals with units (e.g., "5 kilometers" not "five kilometers").

## Citations and Attribution

All specific data, quotations, and non-obvious factual claims must be attributed to their source using the Lantern Press citation format: Author (Year) in running text, with full bibliographic details in chapter endnotes.

When using information from multiple sources to construct a synthesis, cite all relevant sources together. Do not present synthesized conclusions as original claims without attribution.

Generated summaries and briefing documents must list all source files consulted during their preparation.

## Formatting

### File Naming
Use lowercase with underscores for all file names. Include chapter number where applicable. Examples: chapter_1_coastal_overview.md, reference_temperature_data.txt.

### Document Format
Submit all manuscripts as Markdown (.md) files. Use standard Markdown formatting for headings, emphasis, lists, and links. Do not use HTML within Markdown files unless absolutely necessary for complex table layouts.
