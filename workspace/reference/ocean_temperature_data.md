# Ocean Temperature Data Summary — 2024 Monitoring Season

This document summarizes sea surface temperature (SST) observations from the 2024 coastal monitoring network maintained by the National Marine Research Institute. Data covers twelve monitoring stations along the eastern seaboard from Nova Scotia to Florida.

## Annual Summary

The 2024 monitoring season recorded the highest mean annual sea surface temperatures since continuous monitoring began in 1985. The annual mean SST across all twelve stations was 18.7 degrees Celsius, representing a 0.4 degree increase over the 2019-2023 five-year mean and a 1.2 degree increase over the 1985-1994 baseline period.

Summer maximum temperatures (June through August) were particularly notable. Seven of the twelve stations recorded their highest-ever summer SST values, with Station 7 (Cape Hatteras) reaching 29.8 degrees Celsius in late July — a full 1.1 degrees above the previous record set in 2021.

## Regional Patterns

### Northern Stations (Nova Scotia to Cape Cod)
Northern stations showed the most pronounced warming trend relative to historical baselines. Station 1 (Halifax Harbor) recorded a mean summer temperature of 15.2 degrees, which would have been typical of stations two hundred kilometers to the south just three decades ago. This northward shift in temperature isotherms has significant implications for species range distributions.

### Mid-Atlantic Stations (Long Island to Cape Hatteras)
The mid-Atlantic region experienced extended periods of anomalously warm water during summer and early autumn. Station 5 (Chesapeake Bay entrance) recorded thirty-seven consecutive days above 26 degrees Celsius, compared to a historical average of nineteen days. These extended warm periods are associated with increased hypoxic events in estuarine waters.

### Southern Stations (Outer Banks to Florida Keys)
Southern stations showed less dramatic departure from historical norms, as these waters are already near the thermal maximum for many coral species. However, Station 10 (Florida Keys) recorded bleaching-threshold temperatures for forty-two days during summer, contributing to the mass bleaching event documented across Caribbean reefs in 2024.

## Implications for the Collection

These temperature data are directly relevant to several chapters in the coastal ecology collection:

1. Chapter 2 (Marine Life) — species distribution shifts and thermal stress on coral communities
2. Chapter 3 (Physical Processes) — thermal expansion contributions to local sea level rise
3. Chapter 5 (Sustainable Fisheries) — northward movement of commercially important species

The data should be cited as: National Marine Research Institute (2024), Eastern Seaboard SST Monitoring Network Annual Report, NMRI Technical Report 2024-03.

## Data Access

Full datasets are available through the NMRI data portal. Monthly averages, daily observations, and station metadata can be downloaded in CSV format. Quality-controlled data from the 2024 season was released on January 15, 2025.

Note: Some stations experienced instrument maintenance periods that created short gaps in the continuous record. These gaps have been filled using interpolation methods described in the NMRI quality control protocols document.
