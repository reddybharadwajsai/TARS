# External Data Sources and Cross-References

This document lists external data sources referenced throughout the coastal ecology collection. Contributors should verify data currency before final publication.

## Primary Data Sources

The National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA) maintains comprehensive datasets on sea surface temperatures, tidal predictions, and coastal erosion rates. Their Coral Reef Watch program provides near-real-time monitoring of thermal stress conditions affecting reefs worldwide.

The Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC) Working Group II report on Impacts, Adaptation, and Vulnerability contains the most authoritative synthesis of climate change effects on coastal systems. The sixth assessment report, published in 2022, should be the primary reference for climate projections cited in the collection.

## Regional Monitoring Programs

The Northeast Fisheries Science Center operates long-term monitoring programs that track changes in marine community composition along the northeastern United States coast. Their trawl survey data, spanning over fifty years, provides one of the longest continuous records of marine biodiversity change in the world.

The Southeast Area Monitoring and Assessment Program (SEAMAP) conducts fisheries-independent surveys across the southeastern United States continental shelf. Their data on juvenile fish abundance in estuarine nursery habitats is particularly relevant to the mangrove and salt marsh sections of the collection.

## Satellite Remote Sensing

Satellite-derived estimates of ocean color, sea surface temperature, and coastal vegetation extent provide synoptic views of coastal ecosystem condition. The Landsat program offers a forty-year archive of coastal imagery at thirty-meter resolution, enabling analysis of long-term changes in shoreline position, wetland extent, and land use patterns.

The European Space Agency's Sentinel satellites provide higher temporal resolution observations that are particularly useful for monitoring rapid changes such as algal blooms, sediment plumes, and storm damage. Data from these missions are freely available through the Copernicus Marine Environment Monitoring Service.
