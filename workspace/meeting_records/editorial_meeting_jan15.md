## Discussion Notes

### Timeline

Sarah opened by reviewing the current timeline. The collection is scheduled for release in late March, which gives us approximately ten weeks of production time. First drafts of all chapters need to be complete by February 15 to allow adequate time for editing, layout, and proofing.

Marcus raised a concern about the coastal chapter. The research on coral bleaching events has been evolving rapidly, and he wants to ensure we include the most recent data from the 2024 monitoring season. Sarah agreed that we should wait for the December monitoring report before finalizing that section, but emphasized that the structural draft should still be ready on time.

### Coastal Chapter Status

The coastal chapter is currently the most complex piece in the collection. It needs to cover coral reef ecosystems, intertidal zones, mangrove forests, and coastal erosion patterns — essentially four sub-topics that each deserve their own article. Marcus proposed splitting it into two chapters: one focused on living systems (reefs, tide pools, mangroves) and another on physical processes (erosion, sedimentation, sea level change).

Priya noted that splitting would affect the word count allocation and potentially the pricing model for the collection. After discussion, the team agreed to keep it as one chapter but organize it with clear section breaks that allow readers to navigate to their area of interest. Marcus will prepare an outline showing how the four sub-topics flow together.

### Photography and Art

Tom presented mock layouts showing three possible arrangements for the coral reef photography spread. The team preferred option B, which uses a full-bleed image on the opening page with smaller reference photos in the margins. Tom will coordinate with the photographer to ensure we have sufficient high-resolution images for this treatment.
